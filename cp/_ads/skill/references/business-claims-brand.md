## Claims we never make

- Medical, injury, pain-relief, rehabilitation, menopause-symptom, bone-density or posture-fixing outcomes (even where national franchisor copy does).
- Weight loss, toning promises, "transform your body", before and after.
- "The best", "the only", "unique" without a verified comparison.
- Sole award winner. "Studio of the Year".
- Invented scarcity ("only 3 spots left"), deadlines, countdowns, "no obligation", "no lock-in" (memberships have a 3-month minimum).
- "No pressure" or "no sales pitch" about the intro: the studio makes a membership offer at the intro (close offers), so this could mislead. Use "No experience needed" instead.
- "Programme fees apply" (rejected wording).
- Anything about a competitor by name.

## Meta policy and Australian consumer law limits

- **Proof per ad:** one main proof, at most one supporting proof. Don't stack the award, 500+ hours and reviews in one ad.
- **Other attributes:** treat parental status, life stage and body type the same way as age and health (be conservative): don't address the viewer as "mums", "new mums" or similar. Show it in the visual or describe who the class suits instead.
- **Experience is not an attribute:** "Never been on a reformer?" or "New to Pilates?" asks about experience with our product, which is fine. Age, health, body, finances, ethnicity, religion, sexuality and similar are not.
- **Personal attributes (Meta):** ads must not assert or imply a personal attribute of the viewer, including age and physical or mental health. "Are you over 50?", "Over 50? This is for you", "Got a sore back?", "Meet seniors" are out. Meta's own policy page lists "Age is just a number. Anyone can now learn..." as acceptable, so "It's never too late to start Pilates" and "Open to all ages and fitness levels" are fine. "Plenty of our members started in their 50s and 60s" only if true and checked.
- **Age-eligibility offers** (the Page's existing "Special offer on now just for over 50's" ads): borderline. Meta lists "A service for teens" among its examples, so naming an age group as the audience can be read as implying the viewer's age. Prefer benefit or all-ages framing in the ad and put any genuine age eligibility in the offer terms or form. Flag the existing ads to Nichola rather than copying them.
- **Personal health and appearance (Meta):** nothing that implies or tries to create negative self-perception; no before and after; no close-ups of body parts as a problem.
- **Health and wellness (Meta):** check the current restricted-goods page before any health-adjacent wording or targeting; audiences for health topics must not be built from sensitive data.
- **ACCC (misleading conduct):** the overall impression must be true. A "free" offer must disclose material conditions up front, not in later fine print. Price must be the full price for what is described. Testimonials and reviews must be genuine and representative. Health or fitness benefit claims need evidence.
- These summaries were read on 11 Nov 2026. Several Meta policy sub-pages were partly truncated: reread the current page before any execution that touches health, age or body language.
