## Principles

- **Diagnose the behaviour first** (Ogilvy behaviour change: identify the barrier, then design the smallest change that removes it). For us the action is "book and turn up to a first class". Common barriers: not knowing what happens, fear of looking silly, not sure it suits their body or age, time, price, commitment.
- **Test principles, not executions** (Ogilvy behavioural testing; Wpromote hypothesis-led testing). Write each concept as "If we show X to people in situation Y, more will book and attend because Z." Change one meaningful thing at a time when the answer matters.
- **Connect social to the real-world visit** (Born Social, Bravissimo: geo-targeted ads judged on in-store outcomes, with city-level incrementality tests). Judge ads on attended intros and members, not leads alone.
- Awards justify examining an agency's thinking, not copying its budgets, production volumes or reported ROAS.

## Creative audiences vs Meta targeting

- Several motivations live inside one local audience. Don't build one ad set per persona; let the creative do the selecting.
- Hard controls with Advantage+ audience: locations, minimum age, excluded custom audiences, language. Age range, gender, interests and lookalikes are suggestions Meta can go beyond. Verify in the actual ad set before claiming anything is restricted.
- Keep the approved geography (postcodes 4556, 4557, 4558, 4575, 4551, 4553, 4555, 4560, 4564, 4572, 4573) unless Nichola approves a change. Exclude current members if a permitted customer list exists.
- Never target or exclude on health, injury or other sensitive data.

## Offer roles

| | A. Free intro class | B. Paid intro pack (now 4 classes for A$40 in the app) |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Remove the first-visit fear and let the instructor create the "this is for me" moment | Let someone feel two or three real classes and the start of a routine before committing |
| Next step | Lead form, then booking link and Tyson's call, then intro, then membership conversation | Must be a real purchase or a clearly labelled enquiry. Route not built as of 11 Nov 2026 |
| Measure | Cost per attended intro, intro-to-member rate | Cost per pack purchase, attendance of all 3, pack-to-member rate |
| Risks | Tyre-kickers; no-shows; leads not called fast | Discount-seekers; cannibalising members or the $99 offer; poor history of paid packs |
| Reason to choose us | Live instructor, 30 minutes built for first-timers | Must be more than price: try levels and formats with live coaching. 4 for $40 is $10 a class, the same as Station's 6 for $60, so price alone won't win |

Rules: one primary offer and one action per ad. Never send a paid promise into a free-intro form. Never imply the free intro includes a longer programme. Retrieve real terms; never invent expiry, eligibility, minimum commitment or "no obligation".

Default sequencing within the monthly ceiling: run A as the always-on engine. Test B only after its terms and route exist, and test it **instead of** another paid trial (the $99 offer) rather than in addition, so budget stays inside the ceiling and the comparison is clean.

## Creative development rules

- Translate features into what the person values: instructor training = confidence you're doing it right; equipment variety = classes that don't get boring and progress; award and reviews = credibility. Lead with one proposition and its best proof.
- Ask: could this copy belong to any Pilates studio? If yes, rewrite with something only we can show.
- Recognisably Club Pilates (logo artwork, palette, real studio) and recognisably local (Sippy Downs, real instructors).
- Native-looking is fine, sloppy isn't: clear footage, clean sound or captions, good framing, readable text.
- Inspect every automated enhancement (text variations, image expansion, music, related media, multi-advertiser ads). Turn off anything that changes approved assets or wording. Never claim all are off without checking each ad.

## Concept bank (original, untested)

**C1. "Your first class, then every class" (offer A, row 1).** Reel: a real intro (instructor shows the reformer), then a normal class with the instructor coaching mid-exercise. @pilates already shows a first-visit walkthrough, so our difference is live coaching after the first visit too. Hook: "Never been on a reformer?" Proof: 30-minute intro; live instructor every class.

**C2. "Someone's watching your form" (offer A, rows 1 to 3).** Close shots of an instructor adjusting feet, a hand cue at the footbar, then the class moving well. Hook: "A real instructor in the room. Every class." Proof: live classes with named instructors.

**C3. "Start at Level 1" (offer A, row 2).** Members of varied ages (consented) on the reformer and chair. Hook: "Level 1 Foundations. Built for new starters." ("It's never too late to start Pilates" is an alternative; recheck the personal-attributes page before using it over an image of an older member.) Body: built for new starters at any age and fitness level, every exercise can be adjusted. Never "Over 50?". Keep any age eligibility in the offer terms, not the hook.

**C4. "More than a reformer" (offer A or B, row 3).** Quick cuts reformer, Wunda chair, springboard. Hook: "Reformer, Wunda chair, springboard. Same class." Proof: owner-confirmed equipment, studio footage.

**C5. "Three classes to find your level" (offer B, rows 1 and 4).** Hook: "Three classes to find your level and your favourite format." Needs real terms. (Avoid "why people stay": an unsupported retention claim.)

## Placement and production specs (check current Meta specs before production)

- Feed: 4:5, 1080x1350. Reels and Stories: 9:16, 1080x1920.
- 9:16 safe zone (Meta unified, March 2026): keep text and logo out of the top 14% and the bottom 35%, 6% each side. Our renderer places Reel text at about y=300 on 1920.
- Captions burned in for sound-off viewing. First 2 seconds carry the hook.
- Preview every placement before saving: cropping, safe areas, subtitles, logo, mobile readability, and that text, visual, CTA and form all describe the same offer.
