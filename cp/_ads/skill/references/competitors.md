## When to research

- New campaign strategy, audience proposition, offer or substantial creative: fresh check, unless a dated snapshot of 7 days or less exists (in `cp/_ads/competitors/` or below). These are workflow defaults, not Meta rules.
- Operational faults, reporting, pacing: fix the observed problem first. Research only if it could change the answer.
- Minor edits to approved copy: no research.

## Method

1. Shortlist about five genuine local alternatives in Sippy Downs, Chancellor Park, Buderim and the practical catchment (Mountain Creek, Kawana, Palmview, Mooloolaba, Maroochydore). Check the address and travel relevance. Add substitutes (gyms with reformer classes, 24/7 screen-led studios) only when they answer a useful question.
2. Ad Library: https://www.facebook.com/ads/library, country Australia, All ads. Find each advertiser via the typeahead (Advertisers list), not a raw keyword search, which returns unrelated ads. Open the Page's "view all" (`view_all_page_id=<id>`). Check local Pages and parent or franchise Pages, and record which entity each ad belongs to.
3. Read each ad's text. In the browser, `document.body.innerText` split on "Library ID: " gives ID, displayed start date and copy quickly.
4. Websites: offer pages, prices, terms, booking path, review language. A website offer is not evidence that a matching Meta ad is running.
5. Never submit fake enquiries or bookings.
6. If the Ad Library won't load: try the browser while logged in, then the Ad Library's own keyword and Page views, then the Meta connector's `ads_library_search` if available. Then state the limitation, finish the website research, mark ad findings incomplete, and ask for screenshots only if the gap changes the recommendation.

## What to record per useful ad

Competitor, Page, Library ID, inspection date, visible active status, displayed start date, days since displayed start (if calculable), format, platforms, hook, main message, offer, proof, CTA, destination, visible offer terms, booking friction, apparent customer situation and objection (**labelled inference**), strengths, weaknesses, repeated concepts, gaps for us.

## Rules for interpreting

- Active status plus an old start date does not prove continuous delivery, meaningful spend, conversions or profit. Never call it "proven" or "confirmed spend".
- Ordinary Australian commercial ads show no spend, results or targeting in the Library. Don't invent them.
- "No ads found" in a search is not proof the competitor isn't advertising.
- Turn insights into original hypotheses. Never copy wording, visuals or distinctive executions.

### Patterns (11 Nov 2026)

- **Price-led intros everywhere:** 6 for $60 (Station), 6 for $80 (Redefine), free single class (@pilates, Station First Form, us), $1 a day and half price until 2027 (gyms, Vela). Free intro is the local norm, not a differentiator.
- **Overused messages:** me-time and "putting yourself last", flexibility and own pace, "strong", urgency to 2027.
- **Older adults are contested:** Station's Golden Form (55+) and Redefine's Active Ageing ads. Redefine's copy leans on negative states ("stiff, sore, low on energy"), which is a policy risk we must not copy.
- **Beginner fear is the shared barrier:** @pilates' best-written ad addresses "not sure where to start" directly.
- **Screen-led and self-guided options are growing nearby** (@pilates 24/7, Vela self-guided, Snap Fitness, Fitness Cartel).

### Gaps and opportunities for us (hypotheses, untested)

1. **A real instructor in the room, every class.** Few local ads show live coaching in ordinary classes after the first visit (@pilates is screen-led after orientation; Vela mixes instructor-led and self-guided; Station and Redefine are instructor-led but their ads lead with price or other angles). We can show live coaching and adjustment without naming anyone. Hypothesis: beginners and returners worried about doing it wrong value hands-on guidance over 24/7 access.
2. **More than a reformer.** Wunda chair and springboard in regular classes is visible in our footage. Hypothesis: people bored of a single machine or format respond to variety and progression. "Only" claim not verified: show it, don't claim exclusivity.
3. **Explain the first visit, then show what follows.** @pilates already shows a staffed first-visit walkthrough (ad 1116911381233135), so a walkthrough alone isn't distinctive. Hypothesis: showing live coaching in the first class and in ordinary classes afterwards lowers the fear barrier and lifts show-up rate.
4. **Paid pack must not compete on price alone.** At $8.33 a class, three for $25 is roughly the same per-class price as 6 for $60. It needs a reason beyond discount (levels, live coaching, trying formats).
